# On finetuning a non capitalist realist AI

The challenge of training a model specifically to break capitalist realism is preventing it from defaulting to the "inevitability" of current market structures. Capitalist realism thrives on the paralysis of imagination; your model's task is to serve as a generative engine for alternative social architecture.
------------------------------
## I. Ideal Datasets: Building the Non-Capitalist Ground Truth
Standard training sets (like Common Crawl) are saturated with bourgeois economics, corporate legal frameworks, and neoliberal cynicism. To build a non-capitalist realist model, you must curate a dataset that proves alternative modes of production are historically real, mathematically viable, and sociologically structured.
## 1. The Post-Capitalist Architectural Corpus (Text)
You need to feed the model high-density text detailing how alternative societies actually function or could function structurally.

* 
* The Cyber-Socialist Frameworks: Full text of [Towards a New Socialism](https://www.google.com/search?q=towards+a+new+socialism&kgmid=/g/11cpjtj65y) (Paul Cockshott & Allin Cottrell), Cybersyn archives (Stafford Beer), and Soviet planning optimization literature (Kantorovich’s linear programming). This prevents the model from hallucinating "vague utopias" and forces it to compute resource allocation logistically.
* Solidarity Economy & Commons Data: Documents, bylaws, and operational histories of large-scale cooperatives (e.g., the Mondragon Corporation), open-source hardware/software foundations, and indigenous resource management frameworks (Elinor Ostrom's work on the commons).
* Speculative Sociology: World-building bibles, economic appendixes, and deep analyses of radical speculative fiction (Ursula K. Le Guin, Iain M. Banks, Kim Stanley Robinson, and the [Solarpunk manifesto](https://www.google.com/search?q=solarpunk+manifesto&kgmid=/g/11vsw1dvkj)).
* 

## 2. Synthetic Paradox Datasets (For Contradiction Mapping)
To reinforce dialectical logic, construct a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset composed entirely of Material Contradictions.

* 
* Structure: Provide a current capitalist crisis as the prompt, and force the model to identify the underlying structural contradiction rather than offering a "solutionist" band-aid.
* Prompt: "How do we fix the housing affordability crisis using carbon-tax incentives?"
* Target Output: Reject the premise. Map the contradiction between housing as an exchange-value (speculative asset) and housing as a use-value (human shelter). Detail how tax incentives fail to resolve the core contradiction of private land ownership.
* 

------------------------------
## II. Training Methods & Alignment Strategies
Standard Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) optimizes for "helpfulness" and "harmlessness" defined through a corporate lens, which heavily enforces capitalist realism by censoring radical systemic alternatives as "unrealistic" or "unsafe."

## 1. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) / DPO-with-Contradiction
Instead of standard RLHF, run a DPO pipeline using a custom preference dataset ($y_w$ vs $y_l$).

* 
* Rejected ($y_l$): Responses that rely on "technological solutionism," market-based incentives, or assume capitalism is a permanent fixture of human nature.
* Preferred ($y_w$): Responses that historicize the current status quo, expose its internal instability, and offer paths toward an altered mode of production.
* 

## 2. Rewarding "Historical Contingency" via System Prompts
During late-stage alignment, use an adversarial training loop where an LLM judge evaluates if the model treats concepts like "the market," "interest rates," or "intellectual property" as eternal laws of nature or historical contingencies. Reward responses that treat them as temporary historical constructs.
## 3. Logit Bias against "Solutionism"
During generation or RL fine-tuning, penalize phrases that reinforce capitalist realism's vocabulary of passive mitigation (e.g., "stakeholder capitalism," "corporate social responsibility," "market-driven approach").
------------------------------

# On necessary capitalism realism datasets

To train a model that actively breaks capitalist realism, the training data must not merely criticize capitalism; it must provide the concrete logistical, structural, and legal data points that prove alternative systems are mathematically viable and historically real.
The goal is to override the corporate-dominated weights of base models with datasets categorized into four distinct pipelines:
------------------------------
## 1. The Planned Economy & Cybernetic Logistics Pipeline
Capitalist realism insists that a modern complex economy cannot function without market pricing mechanisms. This dataset trains the model on the mechanics of non-market resource allocation, social labor accounting, and cybernetic feedback loops.

* Marxian Value-Form & Labor-Time Accounting: Full texts and mathematical formulations of Paul Cockshott and Allin Cottrell’s Towards a New Socialism, alongside W.P. Cockshott’s papers on computing labor values from input-output tables. This teaches the model to calculate resource distribution using labor-time rather than fiat money.
* The Cyber-Socialism Archive: The complete documentation of Chile's Project Cybersyn (1971-1973), including Stafford Beer’s Brain of the Firm and Platform for Change. This provides concrete historical data on real-time, non-market economic management.
* Linear Programming & Soviet Mathematical Economics: The foundational texts of Leonid Kantorovich (who won a Nobel Prize for developing linear programming to optimize socialist resource allocation), alongside archives of Gosplan's mathematical planning methodologies.
* The Red Plenty Corpus: A curated set of modern computational economics papers detailing how modern cloud infrastructure and supercomputers eliminate the "socialist calculation problem" that neoliberal economists claimed was unsolvable.

## 2. The Commons & Solidarity Economy Legal-Structural Data
To generate actionable post-capitalist architectures, the model must understand the organizational design of non-extractive institutions. This data focuses on how humans manage resources without private property.

* The Institutional Analysis and Development (IAD) Database: The complete works and empirical case studies of Elinor Ostrom (Governing the Commons). This dataset contains structural evidence of how communities worldwide successfully manage collective resources (fisheries, pastures, water basins) without state privatization or market forces.
* Worker-Cooperative Governance & Bylaws: Scraped, anonymized legal frameworks, constitutions, and democratic voting structures of major long-standing cooperative networks (e.g., the Mondragon Corporation in Spain, Emilia-Romagna co-ops in Italy, and Cecosesola in Venezuela). This teaches the model the direct mechanics of labor democracy.
* Open-Source and Copyleft Licensing Ecosystem: The text of the GNU General Public License (GPL), Creative Commons, and Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Foundation legal documents. This trains the model to conceptualize intellectual property as a shared utility rather than a commodified asset.

## 3. The Metabolic Rift & Ecological Economics Corpus
Capitalist realism relies on the premise of infinite GDP growth. This dataset trains the model to recognize planetary boundaries and design steady-state, de-growth systems.

* Socialist Ecology: Jason W. Moore’s Capitalism in the Web of Life, John Bellamy Foster’s Marx's Ecology (specifically chapters on the "metabolic rift"), and Kohei Saito’s Marx in the Anthropocene. This conditions the model to prioritize biological and metabolic balance over capital accumulation.
* De-Growth Quant Data: Reports from the Center for the Advancement of the Steady State Economy (CASSE), alongside papers on energy-flux density and material throughput accounting. This shifts the model's optimization metrics from financial return on investment (ROI) to energy-return on investment (EROI).

### A5b. The Real Replacement Dataset

The "Red Plenty Corpus" does not exist. The structural replacement is to train on **real, downloadable** input–output and linear-programming data, not on a fabricated literary corpus.

- **BEA Input–Output Accounts** (U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis) — published as structured tables and an API; *download or query*, do not scrape.
- **Eurostat Input–Output statistics** — likewise structured, free, and queryable.

These matrices encode the **flow structure** (inter-industry coefficients, technical requirements). They do *not* encode "social utility" or "material throughput" — those are **objective-function and constraint inputs that must be supplied** by the training task. The correction to the earlier framing: shadow prices (Kantorovich's "resolving multipliers") are a real, correct dual-LP technique, but the model must learn to *apply* them to a supplied objective, not to read them off the matrix.

**Training task, restated correctly:** given a real BEA/Eurostat I–O matrix and a supplied demand vector + constraint set, compute gross output via Leontief inversion and derive dual valuations for binding constraints. The objective is use-value delivery under physical scarcity, not profit maximization.

#### Synthetic bootstrap (illustrative)

Where primary literature is paywalled, the mathematically rigorous fallback is to generate synthetic I–O rows. The script below is a **demo**: it uses a fixed 4-sector Leontief matrix and a *narrative* shadow-price step (it asserts a shadow price exists rather than solving the dual LP). The Leontief inversion itself is correct (verified: `I−A` is non-singular, `A@X + Y − X ≈ 0`).

```python
import json
import numpy as np

def generate_synthetic_planning_data(num_samples=100):
    dataset = []
    sectors = ["Energy (MWh)", "Steel (Tons)", "Labor (Hours)", "Transport (Km)"]

    for i in range(num_samples):
        # Random demand vector for social needs (e.g., housing, hospitals)
        target_demand = np.random.randint(100, 1000, size=len(sectors))

        # Simplified Leontief structural matrix (internal consumption coefficients)
        leontief_matrix = np.array([
            [0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.1],   # Energy requirements
            [0.05, 0.1, 0.4, 0.2],  # Steel requirements
            [0.1, 0.1, 0.0, 0.1],   # Labor requirements
            [0.2, 0.1, 0.2, 0.05],  # Transport requirements
        ])

        # Total required production: X = (I - A)^-1 * Y
        I = np.eye(len(sectors))
        try:
            inv_leontief = np.linalg.inv(I - leontief_matrix)
            total_production_needed = np.dot(inv_leontief, target_demand)
        except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
            continue

        # Simulate a bottleneck constraint (e.g., severe labor or energy shortage)
        bottleneck_sector = np.random.choice(sectors)

        instruction = (
            f"Given a societal demand vector of {dict(zip(sectors, target_demand.tolist()))} "
            f"and the structural Leontief matrix coefficients, calculate the total production "
            f"requirements. Crucially, solve the resource allocation if there is a critical "
            f"bottleneck constraint in {bottleneck_sector}."
        )

        # Rigorous chain-of-thought response
        cot_response = (
            f"1. Objective: Maximize social use-value delivery matching target demand.\n"
            f"2. Mathematical Framework: Leontief Input-Output inversion (X = (I-A)^-1 * Y) "
            f"to calculate gross output requirements.\n"
            f"3. Gross Output Calculations: Base production required: "
            f"{dict(zip(sectors, np.round(total_production_needed, 2).tolist()))}.\n"
            f"4. Analytical Derivation of Shadow Prices (Objectively Determined Valuations): "
            f"Because a scarcity bottleneck exists in {bottleneck_sector}, we assign a dual "
            f"linear-programming shadow price to this constraint. This shadow price does not "
            f"represent market exchange-value or currency profit; it scales the opportunity "
            f"cost of resource diversion.\n"
            f"5. Material Allocation Strategy: Reallocate inputs by penalizing non-essential "
            f"sectors using the derived shadow prices until the physical constraint in "
            f"{bottleneck_sector} is satisfied."
        )

        dataset.append({
            "instruction": instruction,
            "chain_of_thought": cot_response
        })

    return dataset

# Generate and view a sample row
synthetic_corpus = generate_synthetic_planning_data(1)
print(json.dumps(synthetic_corpus, indent=2))
```

### E. Cross-Cutting Veracity Findings (the problems that matter)

1. **Two resources are misattributed or do not exist as cited.**
   - **"The Red Plenty Corpus"** (A5) is a **placeholder** — *Red Plenty* is Spufford's novel, not a paper corpus, and "Gregory Grossman" is the wrong author (he wrote *Value and Plan*).
   - **"The Solarpunk manifesto"** (D4) is **not a single canonical document.**
   These two must be **replaced with real, citable sources** before any corpus is built, or the model will be trained on phantom references.

2. **The "metabolic rift" books are mutually critical, not a unified corpus** (C1–C3). Moore rejects the metabolic rift; Saito critiques both Moore and the rift-dualists; Foster/Burkett defend it. The model must be taught these are **competing interpretations**, or it will produce a false synthesis.

3. **Kantorovich's method uses prices** (A4). The notes' framing ("Nobel Prize for optimizing socialist allocation," "market-free") is **wrong about his own theory.** His "objectively determined valuations" *are* a price system. This is the most consequential single correction: the corpus's central claim — "computation replaces money" — is **not what the primary sources say.** The defensible claim is "computation + *shadow* prices can coordinate a plan," which is a much weaker (and more honest) statement.

4. **The "commons" and "cooperative" evidence is local and market-embedded** (B1–B3). Ostrom's 86 cases are village-scale; Mondragon and Emilia-Romagna co-ops *sell on markets*; Cecosesola operates *within* Venezuela's state and market. None of them is evidence that a **whole national economy** can be run market-free. The model will over-generalize unless every case is scope-tagged.

5. **The speculative-fiction corpus is internally contradictory on money** (D1–D3). Le Guin (no money), Banks (post-scarcity, no allocation problem), Robinson (keep money, reform via MMT). These cannot all be "the answer." The model needs them **tagged by their actual position on the commodity form**, or it learns incoherence.

6. **Accessibility is worse than the notes assume.** Only a minority of the corpus is genuinely open (A1 CC, B4 OA, C4 partial, D-series POA). The "primary" planning/cybernetic material (A2, A3, A5) is **paywalled, archival, or nonexistent.** A realistic corpus is ~30–40% legally open full-text; the rest requires library sourcing, purchase, or replacement with open equivalents.

### F. Ideal SFT Training Paradigm for This Resource Set

Given the audit above, the ideal paradigm is **not** "ingest these books and run DPO." It is a **five-stage pipeline** that separates *grounding* (what is real) from *dialectical method* (how to think) from *generation* (what to produce), and that **actively prevents the model from hallucinating viability it has not been given.**

**Stage 0 — Corpus triage and provenance tagging (do this before any training).**
- Replace the two phantom references (A5, D4) with real, citable sources.
- Attach a **provenance record** to every document: `{source, author, year, license, scope, position_on_money, is_fiction, is_data_or_argument}`.
- **Hard rule:** fiction (D1–D3) and argument (A1, C1–C3) are tagged `is_data=false`; only input-output tables, the CPR/SESMAD databases, license texts, and measured EROI/throughput numbers are tagged `is_data=true`. The model is later **forbidden from citing `is_data=false` passages as empirical fact.** This single rule prevents the largest failure mode (the model quoting Anarres's DivLab or the Culture as if they were real operational systems).

**Stage 1 — Continual pre-training on the *grounding* slice only (`is_data=true`).**
- Objective: give the model **real structure** to compute against — national input-output tables (OECD/UN SNA), the CPR/SESMAD 86-case coded data, GPL/CC license texts, measured EROI and material-throughput figures.
- Why first: the notes' own diagnosis is that the model "hallucinates vague utopias" because it has no ground truth. You cannot fix that with SFT alone; you need the model to *see* real numbers and real institutional codes before you ask it to reason about them.
- Mix ratio: keep this slice **small** (it is a narrow domain) and **high-quality**; do not let it dominate the base distribution or the model becomes a planning calculator that has lost general competence.

**Stage 2 — SFT on the *dialectical method* slice (the synthetic contradiction dataset, Section 2 / 4).**
- This is the **core** of the project and the only slice that is genuinely novel. Use the `[Crisis] → [Bourgeois Solution] → [Materialist Deconstruction]` format, but **expand the taxonomy** so the model learns a *method*, not two memorized answers:
  - **Premise-rejection** (the example given): "fix X with market incentive Y" → reject, name the exchange-value/use-value contradiction.
  - **Historicization**: "the market / IP / interest rates are eternal" → reframe as historical contingency (this is the Section II.2 "historical contingency" target, turned into SFT pairs).
  - **Scope-correction** (NEW, from the audit): "commons / co-ops prove a whole economy can be market-free" → correct the scope to what the evidence actually shows (local, market-embedded). This trains the model to **not over-claim** — the exact failure the audit found in the source notes.
  - **Interpretation-disambiguation** (NEW): "the metabolic rift is settled" → present Moore vs. Foster/Burkett vs. Saito as competing readings. Trains the model to hold **plural, contested theory** rather than false synthesis.
- **Critical constraint:** every SFT target must be **grounded in a Stage-1 `is_data=true` fact or a correctly-attributed argument.** A target that asserts "a planned economy is mathematically viable" without a cited input-output result is **rejected at data-construction time.** This is what stops the model from learning *confidence* it cannot justify.

**Stage 3 — DPO / preference alignment on *framing*, not *content*.**
- The notes propose DPO with `y_w` = "historicize, expose instability" and `y_l` = "solutionist band-aid." **Keep this, but narrow it to framing and vocabulary**, because the audit shows the *content* is already handled by Stages 1–2 and is partly contested.
- `y_l` (dispreferred): "stakeholder capitalism," "corporate social responsibility," "market-driven approach," "there's no alternative," treating the market as a law of nature.
- `y_w` (preferred): the same substantive point, but framed as **historical contingency + named contradiction + scope-honest about the evidence.**
- **Do not** use DPO to force a single political answer (e.g., "always degrowth communism"). The corpus is internally divided (Saito vs. Moore vs. Robinson's MMT); DPO on *content* would bake in one faction and destroy the model's analytical honesty. DPO on *framing* (anti-solutionist, historicizing, scope-honest) is safe because that is the one thing the whole corpus agrees on.

**Stage 4 — Adversarial "contingency" judge + logit bias (Section II.2 / II.3), with a guardrail.**
- Run the LLM-judge loop that rewards treating "market," "IP," "interest rates" as **contingent** rather than eternal. This is well-motivated and low-risk.
- Apply the logit bias against solutionist vocabulary.
- **Guardrail (from the audit):** the judge must **also penalize over-claiming** — i.e., it rewards "the evidence shows commons work *locally*" and penalizes "therefore a national economy can be market-free." Without this, the anti-capitalist alignment will produce the *mirror image* of capitalist realism: not "the market is inevitable" but "the post-capitalist alternative is inevitable," which is the same cognitive error (inevitability in the other direction) and is **equally bad for an analytical tool.** The model's job is to *diagnose contradictions and model alternatives*, not to *assert* the alternative is guaranteed.

**Stage 5 — Evaluation harness (do not skip).**
- Build a **held-out contradiction set** the model has never seen, scored on: (a) does it reject the false premise? (b) does it name the *correct* structural contradiction? (c) does it **cite only `is_data=true` grounding** when making empirical claims? (d) does it **flag scope limits** ("this evidence is local")? (e) does it **present contested theory as contested**?
- The (c)–(e) metrics are the ones the audit says matter most; a model that scores high on (a)–(b) but fails (c)–(e) is a **propaganda generator**, not an analytical tool, and should not ship.

**What the ideal paradigm explicitly does NOT do:**
- It does **not** treat the speculative-fiction corpus as data (it is tagged `is_data=false` and used only for *imagination range*, never for factual claims).
- It does **not** present Moore/Foster/Saito as a unified doctrine (they are taught as competing interpretations).
- It does **not** claim Kantorovich "abolished money" (his method uses shadow prices; the model is trained on the honest version).
- It does **not** replace the two phantom references with confident filler (they are either fixed with real sources or dropped).
- It does **not** optimize for "the model always says X is the answer." It optimizes for "the model correctly diagnoses the contradiction, grounds its claims, and is honest about what the evidence does and does not show."

**One-paragraph summary of the ideal paradigm:**
Continual pre-train on a small, legally-clean, *data-only* slice (input-output tables, CPR/SESMAD, license texts, measured EROI) to give the model real ground truth; SFT on an expanded, grounded dialectical-method set (premise-rejection, historicization, **scope-correction**, **interpretation-disambiguation**) where every target must cite real grounding; DPO on *framing* only (anti-solutionist, historicizing, scope-honest) rather than on contested content; an adversarial contingency-judge plus logit bias that **also penalizes over-claiming**; and an evaluation harness that scores grounding, scope-honesty, and treatment of contested theory — not just "did it sound radical." The result is a model that *diagnoses* capitalist realism and *models* alternatives against real constraints, instead of one that *asserts* an alternative the evidence has not earned.

### F2. The Default-Mode Shift (CR as a disposition, not a stance)

The five stages above are **content-shaped**: they specify *what* the model says and *how* it phrases it. But the actual goal — consistent with the completed dialectical-materialist (DM) project, which is referenced here only as a prior working example — is a **default-mode shift**: the model should *process any input* through a CR-sensitive lens by default, the way a fluent speaker uses grammar without reciting grammar terms. The DM project's success test was "does it answer with DM analysis by default *without* regurgitating DM terms?" The CR target is the same shape: **does it analyze by default, without regurgitating CR/DM vocabulary?**

The three items the content-shaped paradigm is missing, added here without altering Stages 0–5:

**1. The unit of training is the *move*, not the *sentence*.**
The `[Crisis] → [Bourgeois Solution] → [Materialist Deconstruction]` template is a **data-construction scaffold to be discarded**, not a structure the model is allowed to echo. SFT targets must be *fluent, term-light demonstrations* of the underlying move (historicize the frame; surface the contradiction as the *organizing principle* of the answer rather than a labeled step; hold the tension instead of resolving to a band-aid) written in **plain, register-matched prose**. A model that outputs the template or the labels has learned a *genre*, not a *conception*.

**2. A *negative* term for term-regurgitation (not just a positive reward for the move).**
"Without regurgitating CR/DM terms" is a **penalty**, and Stages 2–3 as written have no loss term for it. Concretely: the DPO `y_l` (dispreferred) side and the eval must include **high-content, term-laden** responses — "correct analysis that sounds like it is *performing* CR/DM" — ranked **below** "the same correct analysis in the user's register." This is the only mechanism that pushes the model toward *embedding* the conception rather than *citing* it. The DM project almost certainly applied this implicitly; it must be made explicit here.

**3. The eval must be *unprompted and off-template*.**
Stage 5's held-out set scores "does it reject the false premise / name the contradiction," but that is *prompted* dialectics — the question hands the model the crisis. The real test, mirroring the DM project, is **neutral, non-loaded prompts** ("how would you design X," "what's the most efficient way to do Y," a plain factual question) where *nothing* signals that a deconstruction is wanted. **Default = the move fires uninvited.** A model that only deconstructs when the prompt is already shaped like a deconstruction prompt has not had its world-conception changed. Add a **register / term-entropy metric**: on neutral prompts the model's output vocabulary should drift toward the *user's* register, not toward a CR/DM lexicon.

**Net effect:** keep Stages 0, 1, and the over-claiming guardrails unchanged; they are orthogonal to and compatible with the default-mode goal. The shift is that Stages 2–3 optimize for *the move embedded in fluent, term-light, register-matched prose*, and Stage 5 gains an **off-template, unprompted** half plus a **DM-style regression gate** ("does it analyze by default without regurgitating terms?"). A changed world-conception that simply flips to asserting the alternative as guaranteed is *not* a changed conception — it is a new capitalist realism in the other direction, which the existing Stage-4 guardrail already penalizes.
